1.  MA5 #8
A store sells a notebook for $4.50. The price of the notebook decreased by 12%. What is the price of the notebook now?
Her answer on 11/11: C
Answer: A. 12% of $4.50 is 0.12 × 4.50 = $0.54. The new price is 4.50 − 0.54 = $3.96. B subtracts 0.12 instead of 12% of the price (the "decreased by" slip). C adds 0.12. D increases by 12%.
Ask her: How did you get $4.62? Is the new price bigger or smaller than $4.50? What is 10% of $4.50, then 2% more?
2.  MA5 #9
Use the grid to answer the question. How many units is the shortest distance from point A to point B if you can only move down or to the right?
Her answer on 11/11: D
Answer: C. Right: from x = −3 to x = 2 is 5 units (3 to reach 0, then 2 more). Down: from y = 4 to y = −2 is 6 units (4 to reach 0, then 2 more). 5 + 6 = 11. 9 and 10 come from missing a unit when crossing 0. Each grid square is half a unit, so counting squares gives 22; count by the axis numbers instead.
Ask her: How many grid squares are between 0 and 1 on the x-axis? So what does one square stand for?
3.  MA5 #15
Maria did the problem 70 ÷ 9 × 124. Approximately what was her answer?
Her answer on 11/11: B
Answer: C. Work left to right: 70 ÷ 9 first. 72 ÷ 9 = 8, so 70 ÷ 9 is a little less than 8 (about 7.8). 7.8 × 124 is about 964: between 900 and 1,200. A comes from doing 9 × 124 first, as if the problem were 70 ÷ (9 × 124). B comes from rounding too much: 70 ÷ 10 × 120 = 840.
Ask her: What is 70 ÷ 9, about? Is it closer to 7 or to 8? Then times 124?
4.  MA5 #16
Three bottles hold 58 liter, 0.6 liter, and 710 liter of water. How much more water does the bottle with the most hold than the bottle with the least?
Her answer on 11/11: A
Answer: C. 5/8 = 0.625 and 7/10 = 0.7. In order: 0.6, 0.625, 0.7. Most minus least: 0.7 − 0.6 = 0.1. A is 0.625 − 0.6 and B is 0.7 − 0.625: each uses the middle bottle. D is 1 − 0.6.
Ask her: Write all three as decimals and put them in order. Which is the most, which is the least?
5.  MA5 #21
Which expression is equivalent to 500 − 9 × (40 − 3)?
Her answer on 11/11: B
Answer: C. 9 × (40 − 3) = 9 × 40 − 9 × 3 = 360 − 27 = 333, and 500 − 333 = 167. Taking it away from 500 flips the second sign: 500 − 360 + 27 = 167. B keeps the minus (500 − 360 − 27 = 113). A multiplies only the 40. D subtracts before multiplying.
Ask her: What is 9 × 37? Now work out 500 − that. Which choice gives the same number?
6.  MA5 #23
Jo has a roll with 3 yards of ribbon and a bag of 24 buttons. She wants to make 12 bookmarks. Each bookmark uses 10 inches of ribbon and 2 buttons. There are 3 feet in 1 yard and 12 inches in 1 foot. Which statement best explains whether Jo has enough ribbon?
Her answer on 11/11: B
Answer: D. 1 yard = 3 × 12 = 36 inches, so 3 yards = 108 inches. 12 bookmarks need 12 × 10 = 120 inches. 108 is less than 120, so she does not have enough. A uses 12 inches in a yard (her QR10 #11 slip): the conclusion happens to be right, but the reason is wrong. B has the right numbers and the wrong conclusion. C is about buttons, which the question does not ask.
Ask her: You have 108 inches and need 120. Do you have enough? Read choice B and D slowly: what is different?
