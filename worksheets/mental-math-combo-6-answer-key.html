15 problems. Built from the actual timing/change-log data across Combo 1, 2, 4, the standalone percent drill, the standalone order-three drill, and the standalone simplify drill — not a guess at what's hard. See each part's note for the specific evidence behind it.

Part 1 — Percent Increase and Decrease

The "decreased/increased BY X%" language trap — confirmed twice (QR8 10/27, MA3 10/30) as treating the instruction like subtracting X directly instead of X% OF the value. Non-shortcut rates (35%, 45%, 15%) on purpose, so the percent-of-a-number step can't be skated through either.

1.
A backpack costs $40. Its price is decreased by 35%. What is the new price, in dollars?
Answer: $26. 35% of 40 = 14 (the DECREASE). New price = 40 − 14 = 26. Not 40 − 0.35.
2.
A greenhouse has 60 plants. The number of plants increases by 45%. How many plants does it have now?
Answer: 87. 45% of 60 = 27 (the INCREASE). New total = 60 + 27 = 87.
3.
A town has 1,500 residents. The population decreases by 15%. How many residents does the DECREASE represent (not the new population)?
Answer: 225. 15% of 1,500 = 225. This question stops at the change itself — don't go an extra step and subtract it from 1,500 (that would answer a different question than the one asked).
Part 2 — Percent of a Number

Still an open gap as of the 10/22 combo drills (45% of 120 wrong on Combo 2, 80% of 45 wrong on Combo 4) — non-shortcut percentages, no 10/25/50% here.

4.
What is 35% of 140?
Answer: 49. 0.35 × 140 = 49.
5.
What is 15% of 220?
Answer: 33. 0.15 × 220 = 33. (Or 10% of 220 = 22, half that is 11, 22+11=33.)
6.
What is 70% of 90?
Answer: 63. 0.7 × 90 = 63. (Or 10% of 90 = 9, ×7 = 63.)
Part 3 — Ordering More Than 2 Values

The single most consistently wrong thing in the whole dataset: wrong on the standalone order-three drill (5 of 8), Combo 1 #9, Combo 2 #9, and Combo 4 #5 — right only once (Combo 4 #6, a 4-value version). Five problems here on purpose, all with close margins so there's no shortcut around actually converting each value.

7.
Order least to greatest: 59, 0.58, 712
Answer: 5/9, 0.58, 7/12. 5/9 ≈ 0.556, 7/12 ≈ 0.583.
8.
Order least to greatest: 0.72, 1115, 57
Answer: 5/7, 0.72, 11/15. 5/7 ≈ 0.714, 11/15 ≈ 0.733.
9.
Order least to greatest: 38, 0.4, 513
Answer: 3/8, 5/13, 0.4. 3/8 = 0.375, 5/13 ≈ 0.385.
10.
Order least to greatest: 0.62, 58, 914
Answer: 0.62, 5/8, 9/14. 5/8 = 0.625, 9/14 ≈ 0.643.
11.
Order least to greatest: 47, 0.56, 916
Answer: 0.56, 9/16, 4/7. 9/16 = 0.5625, 4/7 ≈ 0.571.

Part 5 — Equation Substitution

Maintenance, not a new gap — she set up Combo 4 #1's equation correctly but still got the final arithmetic wrong (wrote 2 instead of 6), so the write-it-down step alone isn't the whole fix.

14.
The formula for the perimeter of a rectangle is P = 2(l + w). A rectangle has a perimeter of 50 ft and a length of 18 ft. What is its width?
Step 1: 50 = 2(18 + w). Final answer: 7. 50 ÷ 2 = 25, 25 − 18 = 7.
Part 6 — Multi-step Unit Conversion

Maintenance — this format has gone right every time it's been tested (Combo 1-2-4 all correct); kept short on purpose.

15.
4 nibs = 20 ploks. 1 plok = 5 quors. How many quors equal 3 nibs?
Step 1: 1 nib = 5 ploks. Final answer: 75. 3 nibs = 15 ploks, and 15 ploks × 5 quors/plok = 75 quors.
