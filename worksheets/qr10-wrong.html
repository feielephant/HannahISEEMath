1.  Set 10 #1
Lena volunteers at an animal shelter and records information about each dog. She made a list of the information that is measured or counted with numbers.
1. Weight of each dog, in pounds
2. Age of each dog, in months
3. ?
Which information best completes Lena’s list?
Her answer on 11/10: B
Answer: A. Weight and age are numbers. Only A is a number (a count of walks). B, C, and D are all categories (words), so the one choice that is different from the other three is the answer.
Ask her: Which of the 4 choices is counted or measured with a number?
2.  Set 10 #4
A baker has 96 cookies. She puts the same number of cookies into each of 8 boxes. If c represents the number of cookies in each box, which equation can be used to find c?
Her answer on 11/10: A
Answer: D. 8 boxes with c cookies each make 96 cookies: 8 × c = 96. That is the same as c = 96 ÷ 8 = 12. A is backwards, the mistake on Practice Test 6 MA #12.
Ask her: Put in 12 cookies per box. Is 96 × 12 = 8 true, or 8 × 12 = 96?
3.  Set 10 #5
The number machine performs the same operation on each input to create an output. What is the output for an input of 30?
NUMBER MACHINE
InputOutput
45
108

Her answer on 11/10: D
Answer: B. Inputs go up by 6 while outputs go up by 3, so the output grows half as fast: try input ÷ 2 + 3. Check: 4 ÷ 2 + 3 = 5, 22 ÷ 2 + 3 = 14. So 30 ÷ 2 + 3 = 18. A treats 30 as the next row (that would be 28). D is 30 + 3, leaving out the half.
Ask her: Does your rule work for input 4? For input 22?
4.  Set 10 #6
A store gave a 20% discount off the regular price of a jacket. The amount of the discount was $7. What was the regular price of the jacket?
Her answer on 11/10: A
Answer: C. $7 is 20% of the regular price. 20% = 0.2, so the price is 7 ÷ 0.2 = 35 (or: 20% is one fifth, so 7 × 5). A is 7 × 2. B is the sale price, 35 − 7.
Ask her: $7 is 20% of the price. 20% is what fraction? So the price is $7 times what?
5.  Set 10 #8
Nina did the problem 50 ÷ 12 × 182. Approximately what was her answer?
Her answer on 11/10: B
Answer: C. Work left to right: 50 ÷ 12 first. 48 ÷ 12 = 4, so 50 ÷ 12 is a little more than 4. 4 × 182 is about 730, and the exact answer is about 758: between 600 and 900. A comes from doing 12 × 182 first. Rounding 12 down to 10 and 182 to 180 gives exactly 900, which sits on the line between C and D, so that rounding is too rough here.
Ask her: What is 50 ÷ 12, about? Then times 182?
6.  Set 10 #11
Mia has a roll with 4 yards of ribbon and a box of 40 beads. She wants to make 15 bows. Each bow uses 10 inches of ribbon and 2 beads. There are 3 feet in 1 yard and 12 inches in 1 foot. Which statement best explains whether Mia has enough ribbon?
Her answer on 11/10: A
Answer: B. 1 yard = 3 × 12 = 36 inches, so 4 yards = 144 inches. 15 bows need 15 × 10 = 150 inches. 144 is less than 150, so she does not have enough. A uses 12 inches in a yard. C has the right numbers and the wrong conclusion. D is about beads, which the question does not ask.
Ask her: How many feet are in 4 yards? How many inches is that?
7.  Set 10 #13
Which list shows the fractions 47, 23, 59, and 35 ordered from greatest to least?
Her answer on 11/10: C
Answer: D. As decimals: 2/3 ≈ 0.667, 3/5 = 0.6, 4/7 ≈ 0.571, 5/9 ≈ 0.556. The close pair is 4/7 and 5/9: cross-multiply, 4 × 9 = 36 and 5 × 7 = 35, so 4/7 is bigger. A is least to greatest (bigger numbers do not mean a bigger fraction). B swaps the close pair. C swaps 2/3 and 3/5.
Ask her: Which is bigger, 2/3 or 3/5? How did you check?
8.  Set 10 #14
Lisa ran 78 mile on Monday, 0.85 mile on Tuesday, and 45 mile on Wednesday. How much farther did she run on her longest day than on her shortest day?
Her answer on 11/10: D
Answer: C. 7/8 = 0.875 and 4/5 = 0.8. In order: 0.8, 0.85, 0.875. Longest minus shortest: 0.875 − 0.8 = 0.075. A is Monday minus Tuesday. B is Tuesday minus Wednesday. D is 1 − 0.875.
Ask her: What is 4/5 as a decimal? Which day is the shortest?
