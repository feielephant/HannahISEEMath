5 Habit 1: Check Every Condition
Problem 1
Which number is divisible by both 4 and 3?
AnswerC (132)
Why: Check BOTH rules for every choice: 124 is divisible by 4 (124÷4=31) but its digits sum to 7, not divisible by 3. 126’s digits sum to 9 (divisible by 3) but 126÷4=31.5, not divisible by 4. 132 passes both: 132÷4=33, and 1+3+2=6. 125 fails both.
Problem 2
Serena is thinking of a number. It is greater than 60 and less than 70. It is odd, and it is divisible by 5. What number is Serena thinking of?
AnswerB (65)
Why: Check all three conditions against every choice: 60 is divisible by 5 but even, and not greater than 60 (it IS 60). 68 is in range but even. 75 is odd and divisible by 5 but outside the 60–70 range. Only 65 passes all three: in range, odd, divisible by 5.
Problem 3
Which number between 20 and 30 leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by BOTH 4 and 6?
AnswerB (25)
Why: Check both divisions for each choice. 25÷4 = 6 remainder 1, and 25÷6 = 4 remainder 1 — passes both. 21÷6 leaves remainder 3 (fails). 26÷4 leaves remainder 2 (fails). 28÷4 leaves remainder 0 (fails).
Problem 4
Maria has more than 30 but fewer than 40 marbles. The number of marbles is a multiple of 6. How many marbles does Maria have?
AnswerC (36)
Why: 30 is a multiple of 6, but the marbles must be MORE than 30, so 30 itself doesn’t count. 42 is a multiple of 6 but is over 40. 33 isn’t a multiple of 6 at all. Only 36 passes both conditions: it’s between 30 and 40, and it’s a multiple of 6.
Problem 5
Which quadrilateral always has all four sides equal AND at least one right angle?
AnswerB (square)
Why: A rhombus has 4 equal sides but not necessarily any right angles. A rectangle and parallelogram don’t necessarily have 4 equal sides. Only a square is guaranteed to have both: 4 equal sides AND 4 right angles.
5 Habit 2: Use Every Piece of Data
Problem 6
Five friends measured their heights in inches: 52, 55, 58, 60, and 65. What is the mean height, in inches?
Answer58
Why: Add ALL five heights: 52+55+58+60+65 = 290. Then 290 ÷ 5 = 58. Count the numbers given (5) and make sure all 5 went into the sum.
Problem 7
A gift basket has 8 apples, 5 oranges, 3 pears, 6 bananas, 4 kiwis, and 2 mangoes. If the fruit is divided evenly into 4 baskets, how many pieces of fruit go in each basket?
Answer7
Why: Add all SIX fruit counts first: 8+5+3+6+4+2 = 28. Then 28 ÷ 4 = 7. It’s easy to add only some of the six numbers by accident — recount how many kinds of fruit are listed (6) before dividing.
Problem 8
The table shows the number of books read by two book clubs last month, by genre. Which genre had the most books read IN TOTAL (both clubs combined)?

GenreClub AClub B
Mystery69
Fantasy112
History45
Sci-Fi76
AnswerA (Mystery)
Why: Add BOTH clubs for every genre, not just one: Mystery = 6+9 = 15, Fantasy = 11+2 = 13, History = 4+5 = 9, Sci-Fi = 7+6 = 13. Mystery has the most combined. Fantasy has the single tallest column (11) for one club, which is the trap if you only check one club’s numbers.
Problem 9
A set of numbers shows the number of emails 7 coworkers received today: 22, 9, 31, 15, 4, 27, and 18. What is the median number of emails?
Answer18
Why: Sort ALL seven values first: 4, 9, 15, 18, 22, 27, 31. With 7 values, the median is the 4th one once sorted: 18. Leaving out any of the 7 values while sorting throws off which one is the middle.
Problem 10
The table shows the number of visitors to a museum on 5 days. Which day had attendance closest to the MEAN (average) across all 5 days?

DayMonTueWedThuFri
Visitors14095210160125
AnswerB (Monday)
Why: Find the mean using ALL 5 days: (140+95+210+160+125) ÷ 5 = 730 ÷ 5 = 146. Now compare every day to 146: Tue is 51 away, Mon is 6 away, Wed is 64 away, Thu is 14 away. Monday is closest. Skipping any day when adding would give the wrong mean to compare against.
5 Habit 3: Watch the Key Word
Problem 11
Which list shows 3/8, 1/2, 5/6, and 1/4 in order from GREATEST to least?
AnswerA (5/6, 1/2, 3/8, 1/4)
Why: As decimals: 5/6≈0.83, 1/2=0.5, 3/8=0.375, 1/4=0.25. GREATEST to least means starting with the biggest: 5/6, 1/2, 3/8, 1/4. Choice B is the exact reverse (least to greatest) — the classic trap for this wording.
Problem 12
Eight campers split the cost of a canoe rental equally. The number of campers is □. The total rental cost is ○. Which expression gives the cost per camper?
AnswerD (○ ÷ □)
Why: “Split equally” among a group means divide the total by the number of people: cost per camper = total ÷ number of campers = ○ ÷ □.
Problem 13
A square has a side length of 7 cm. A rectangle has the same AREA as the square, and a length of 14 cm. What is the width of the rectangle, in centimeters?
Answer3.5
Why: The square’s AREA is 7×7=49 sq cm. The rectangle has the same area, so its width is 49÷14 = 3.5 cm. Notice this problem says area, not perimeter.
Problem 14
A square has a side length of 7 cm. A rectangle has the same PERIMETER as the square, and a length of 12 cm. What is the width of the rectangle, in centimeters?
Answer2
Why: The square’s PERIMETER is 4×7=28 cm. The rectangle has the same perimeter (not the same area — this is the same square as the last problem, but a different word!): 2(12+width)=28, so 12+width=14, and width=2 cm.
Problem 15
Which number is NOT divisible by 6?
AnswerD (28)
Why: Watch the word NOT. 18, 24, and 30 are all divisible by 6 (18÷6=3, 24÷6=4, 30÷6=5). 28÷6 = 4 remainder 4, so 28 is the one that is NOT divisible by 6.
5 Habit 4: Answer the Exact Thing Asked
Problem 16
In the number 84,657, what digit is in the HUNDREDS place?
AnswerC (6)
Why: Break down the places: 8 = ten-thousands, 4 = thousands, 6 = hundreds, 5 = tens, 7 = ones. The question asks specifically for the hundreds digit: 6. (8 is the ten-thousands digit — a common mix-up when the question asks for a specific place.)
Problem 17
The perimeter of the largest polygon is 30 centimeters. The perimeter of the square is 20 centimeters. What is the perimeter of the triangle?
AnswerC (20)
Why: Square’s side = 20÷4 = 5 cm. Triangle’s perimeter = 30 − 20 + 2×5 = 20 cm. Choice A (10 = 30−20) is the trap for stopping after the first subtraction instead of finishing the calculation — that’s an intermediate number, not the final answer.
Problem 18
A bag has 20 marbles: 8 red, 5 blue, and 7 green. What is the probability of NOT drawing a red marble?
AnswerB (3/5)
Why: “NOT red” means blue or green: 5+7=12 out of 20, which is 3/5. Choice A (2/5 = 8/20) is the probability of drawing red — the opposite of what was asked.
Problem 19
Tom buys a shirt for $18 and a hat for $12. He pays with a $50 bill. How much change does he get?
AnswerB ($20)
Why: Total cost = 18+12 = $30. Change = 50 − 30 = $20. Choice A ($30) is the total cost, an intermediate number — the question asked for the change, not the cost.
Problem 20
A set of test scores: 70, 85, 90, 75, and 95. What is the MEDIAN score?
AnswerB (85)
Why: Sort first: 70, 75, 85, 90, 95. The median (middle value) is 85. Choice A (83) is the MEAN (415÷5=83) — a different measure than what was asked for.
5 Habit 5: Compute Both Sides, Do Not Assume
Problem 21
Room A is a rectangle with a length of 10 ft and a width of 4 ft. Room B is a square with a side of 7 ft. Which statement is true?
AnswerC (The two rooms have equal perimeters.)
Why: Room A: area = 10×4=40, perimeter = 2(10+4)=28. Room B: area = 7×7=49, perimeter = 4×7=28. The perimeters come out exactly equal (28=28), even though the areas are different (40 vs 49). You have to compute both to see this — you can’t assume from the shapes alone.
Problem 22
Two numbers are greater than 1. One is even and the other is odd. What must always be true about their SUM?
AnswerB (always odd)
Why: Even + odd always gives an odd result (test it: 4+3=7, 10+5=15). It’s tempting to assume the sum could be either, but it’s worth actually testing an example rather than guessing.
Problem 23
Which statement is true?
AnswerB (A trapezoid is always a quadrilateral.)
Why: A trapezoid is defined as a 4-sided shape, so every trapezoid is a quadrilateral — that direction always holds. The other three reverse the relationship or mix up two different shape types; they sound plausible but fail when you actually check a specific example.
Problem 24
Which is greater: 5/8 of 40, or 3/5 of 60?
AnswerB (3/5 of 60 is greater)
Why: 5/8 of 40 = 25. 3/5 of 60 = 36. You have to actually calculate both values — you can’t tell just from comparing the fractions 5/8 and 3/5, since the two starting numbers (40 and 60) are different.
Problem 25
Rectangle A is 3 ft by 20 ft. Rectangle B is 8 ft by 8 ft. Which statement is true?
AnswerB (Rectangle A has the greater perimeter.)
Why: Rectangle A: area = 3×20=60, perimeter = 2(3+20)=46. Rectangle B: area = 8×8=64, perimeter = 2(8+8)=32. Rectangle B actually has the LARGER area (64 > 60) but the SMALLER perimeter (32 < 46) — a long, thin rectangle can have a smaller area but a bigger perimeter than a square. This only shows up if you compute both instead of assuming the bigger-looking shape wins both.
5 Knowledge Gap Check
Problem 26
The table shows the number of votes for favorite school mascots. Based on the table, what is the most reasonable prediction of the number of votes for Wildcat out of the next 20 votes?

MascotFalconWildcatBearEagle
Votes1428632
AnswerC (7)
Why: Total votes = 14+28+6+32 = 80. Wildcat is 28 of 80, which is 0.35 (35%). Applying that rate to 20 more votes: 0.35 × 20 = 7. Choice B (22) comes from subtracting two table values (28−6) instead of building a rate from the total — a real signal of not yet treating this as a proportion problem.
Problem 27
A solid is made by joining two rectangular boxes with no overlap. The first box is 3 ft by 3 ft by 8 ft. The second box is 3 ft by 2 ft by 5 ft. What is the total volume of the solid, in cubic feet?
Answer102
Why: Volume of the first box = 3×3×8 = 72 cubic ft. Volume of the second box = 3×2×5 = 30 cubic ft. Total = 72+30 = 102 cubic ft. This checks whether composite 3D shapes (reading and combining two sets of dimensions correctly) are secure.
Problem 28
Look at this pattern: 3, 4, 6, 10, 18, ___. What is the next number in the pattern?
Answer34
Why: The differences between consecutive numbers double each time: 4−3=1, 6−4=2, 10−6=4, 18−10=8. The next difference should be 16, so 18+16=34. This checks whether she catches a growth rule that isn’t just "add the same amount" every time.
Problem 29
Two ribbons are being joined together. The first ribbon is 2/5 of a yard long, and the second ribbon is 1/2 of a yard long. Will the combined ribbon be longer than 1 yard, shorter than 1 yard, or exactly 1 yard?
AnswerB (Shorter than 1 yard)
Why: 2/5 + 1/2 = 4/10 + 5/10 = 9/10, which is less than 1 (10/10). So the combined ribbon is shorter than 1 yard. This checks whether adding unlike-denominator fractions and comparing the result to a whole number is secure.
Problem 30
A shipping box contains 40 unit cubes, but there are gaps of empty space between some of the cubes. Which statement is true about the volume of the box?
AnswerC (The volume is greater than 40 cubic units.)
Why: Volume is the total space inside the box, including any gaps — not just the space the cubes themselves take up. Since there is empty space between cubes in addition to the 40 cubic units of the cubes, the box’s volume is greater than 40. This checks whether "volume" is understood as total enclosed space, not just "how many solid cubes."
