3 Sticky Gap: Predicting From a Ratio
Problem 1
The table shows how 60 students voted for their favorite pizza topping. Based on this sample, about how many of a NEW group of 45 students would you expect to choose Pepperoni?
ToppingCheesePepperoniVeggieSausage
Votes1524912
AnswerD (18)
Why: Total votes = 15+24+9+12 = 60. Pepperoni got 24 of 60, which is 0.4 (40%). A NEW group is expected to follow roughly the same pattern, so 0.4 × 45 = 18. Choice A (12 = 24−12) and choice B (15 = 24−9) both come from subtracting two table values instead of building a rate — the same trap as before.
Problem 2
A bag has 50 marbles, and 35 of them are blue. If a new bag has 20 marbles from the exact same mix, about how many would you expect to be blue?
Answer14
Why: 35 of 50 is 35/50 = 0.7 (70%). A new, smaller bag from the same mix should still be about 70% blue: 0.7 × 20 = 14. The key idea: a new sample is expected to roughly match the pattern of the original sample, just scaled to its own size.
Problem 3
A survey of 90 shoppers found that 18 preferred online shopping over in-store. Based on this, about how many of the next 55 shoppers would be expected to prefer online shopping?
Answer11
Why: 18 of 90 is 18/90 = 0.2 (20%). Apply that same rate to the new group: 0.2 × 55 = 11. Before calculating, ask: "is this the same kind of situation as before?" — a sample rate, applied to a new group size.
3 Sticky Gap: Two Volumes, Joined, Means Add Them
Problem 4
A solid figure is made by attaching two rectangular boxes together with NO overlapping space. Box 1 measures 4 ft by 2 ft by 5 ft. Box 2 measures 4 ft by 3 ft by 2 ft. Since the boxes do not overlap, the total volume is the volume of Box 1 PLUS the volume of Box 2. What is the total volume, in cubic feet?
Answer64
Why: Box 1: 4×2×5 = 40 cubic ft. Box 2: 4×3×2 = 24 cubic ft. Because the boxes don’t overlap, NO space is shared between them, so the total volume is simply 40+24 = 64 cubic ft. Whenever two solids are joined without overlapping, their volumes always add together.
Problem 5
A solid is formed by joining two rectangular prisms with no overlap. The first prism is 6 cm by 3 cm by 4 cm. The second prism is 6 cm by 2 cm by 3 cm. What is the total volume of the solid, in cubic centimeters?
Answer108
Why: First prism: 6×3×4 = 72 cubic cm. Second prism: 6×2×3 = 36 cubic cm. No overlap means the volumes add: 72+36 = 108 cubic cm.
Problem 6
A moving container is built from two connected rectangular sections with no overlapping space. The first section is 5 ft by 4 ft by 6 ft. The second section is 5 ft by 4 ft by 2 ft. What is the total volume of the moving container, in cubic feet?
Answer160
Why: First section: 5×4×6 = 120 cubic ft. Second section: 5×4×2 = 40 cubic ft. No overlap means add the two volumes: 120+40 = 160 cubic ft. The pattern is always the same: find each piece’s volume separately, then add.
3 Sticky Gap: Trust the Math, Even When It Is Not a Whole Number
Problem 7
A square has a side length of 9 cm. A rectangle has the same AREA as the square, and a length of 20 cm. What is the width of the rectangle, in centimeters?
Answer4.05
Why: Area of the square = 9×9 = 81 sq cm. Width of the rectangle = 81 ÷ 20 = 4.05 cm. This does NOT divide evenly, and that is completely fine — it does not mean you made a mistake. Many real measurements come out as decimals. If your method is right, write down the exact answer, even when it is not a whole number.
Problem 8
A square has a side length of 5 in. A rectangle has the same AREA as the square, and a length of 8 in. What is the width of the rectangle, in inches?
Answer3.125
Why: Area of the square = 5×5 = 25 sq in. Width = 25 ÷ 8 = 3.125 in (or 3 1/8 in). Same reminder as the last problem: a non-whole-number result is a normal, correct answer here — keep going instead of assuming you went wrong.
Problem 9
Notebooks are sold in packs of 8 for $9.00. What is the price per notebook, in dollars?
Answer1.125
Why: 9.00 ÷ 8 = 1.125, or $1.125 per notebook (this is why store prices often end in a fraction of a cent before rounding). Trust the division even though it does not come out to a round number.
6 Multi-Habit Problems
Problem 10
Which number is EVEN and is NOT divisible by 5?
AnswerB (14)
Why: Check BOTH conditions for every choice, and watch the word NOT. 20 is even, but 20÷5=4 exactly, so it IS divisible by 5 (fails the NOT). 30 is even but also divisible by 5 (fails). 15 is divisible by 5 but it is odd (fails the even condition). Only 14 is even AND not divisible by 5 (14÷5=2 remainder 4). This combines Habit 1 (check every condition) with Habit 3 (watch the word NOT).
Problem 11
The table shows the number of steps 5 friends walked in a day. What is the DIFFERENCE between the highest and lowest number of steps?
AmirBellaChenDanaElle
8,2006,5009,1007,3005,900
Answer3200
Why: Use ALL 5 values to find the highest (Chen, 9,100) and the lowest (Elle, 5,900). The question asks for the DIFFERENCE, which is the final answer, not just naming the two friends: 9,100−5,900 = 3,200. This combines Habit 2 (use every value to find the true max and min) with Habit 4 (the difference is the exact thing asked for, not the two numbers themselves).
Problem 12
A square has an area of 64 square feet. A rectangle has a length of 20 feet and a width of 4 feet. Which statement is true?
AnswerA (The rectangle has the greater perimeter.)
Why: Square: area 64 means side = 8 ft (since 8×8=64), so perimeter = 4×8 = 32 ft. Rectangle: area = 20×4 = 80 sq ft, perimeter = 2(20+4) = 48 ft. This combines Habit 3 (the question gives area but asks about perimeter — you have to convert first) with Habit 5 (compute both perimeters instead of assuming): 48 > 32, so the rectangle has the greater perimeter.
Problem 13
One number is even and greater than 10. Another number is odd and greater than 10. What must always be true about their SUM?
AnswerC (always odd)
Why: The "greater than 10" parts are extra conditions that don’t actually matter for this question — only even + odd matters, which always gives an odd sum (test it: 12+11=23, 18+15=33). This combines Habit 1 (figuring out which conditions actually matter) with Habit 5 (testing real examples instead of assuming): choice B fails on 18+15=33 (not prime), and D fails on 12+11=23 (not a multiple of 3).
Problem 14
The table shows the number of fish caught by 4 friends. What is the total number of fish caught by the two friends who caught the FEWEST fish?
SamTiaUmaVik
127159
Answer16
Why: Use all 4 values to rank them: Sam 12, Tia 7, Uma 15, Vik 9. The two friends who caught the FEWEST fish are Tia (7) and Vik (9) — not just the single fewest. The question asks for their combined total: 7+9=16. This combines Habit 2 (use every value to rank correctly), Habit 3 (watch "fewest," and notice it asks for TWO friends, not one), and Habit 4 (the final answer is their sum, not their names).
Problem 15
A rectangle has a perimeter of 26 inches and a width of 5 inches. What is the AREA of the rectangle, in square inches?
Answer40
Why: Perimeter is given, but AREA is asked — a key-word switch (Habit 3). First find the length: 2(length+5)=26, so length=8. Then find area (Habit 4, the actual thing asked): 8×5=40 sq in. Stopping at length=8 (an intermediate step) is the trap this problem is built to catch.
