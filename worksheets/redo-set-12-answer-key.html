2 Sticky Gap: A Forced Digit From Carrying
Problem 1
Each letter represents a different digit from 1 through 9: PP + QQ = RST. What digit does R represent?
Answer1
Why: PP is really 11×p and QQ is 11×q, so the sum is 11×(p+q). Since p and q are different digits from 1–9, the biggest p+q can be is 8+9=17 and the smallest that still makes a 3-digit result is 10 (11×9=99 is only 2 digits). Every value from 11×10=110 up to 11×17=187 starts with a 1 — so no matter which digits p and q actually are, R is always 1.
Problem 2
Each letter represents a different digit from 1 through 9: MM + NN = XYZ. What digit does X represent?
Answer1
Why: Same idea as a repeated-digit addition like this one: MM is 11×m and NN is 11×n, so the sum is always 11×(m+n). For the result to have 3 digits at all, m+n must be at least 10, and it can be at most 17. Every multiple of 11 from 110 to 187 has a 1 in the hundreds place, so X is always 1 — the specific digits chosen for M and N don’t change that.
2 Sticky Gap: Matching Each Symbol to Its Quantity
Problem 3
Maria had ▲ stickers and then gave ● of them to her sister. Now, her sister has ■ times as many stickers as Maria had to begin. Which expression represents the number of stickers the sister had to begin with?
AnswerA (■×▲−●)
Why: Work out what each symbol actually stands for before combining them: ▲ is Maria’s ORIGINAL amount, ● is the amount she gave away, and ■ is the multiplier. The sister’s amount NOW is ■×▲ (the multiplier times Maria’s original amount, not the amount given away). But the sister’s amount now also equals her starting amount plus the ● she received, so her starting amount is ■×▲−●. Swapping which symbol plays which role (using ● where ▲ belongs, or the reverse) is the trap in the other three choices.
Problem 4
Ben had ◆ marbles and then gave ★ of them to his cousin. Now, his cousin has ⬟ times as many marbles as Ben had to begin. Which expression represents the number of marbles the cousin had to begin with?
AnswerB (⬟×◆−★)
Why: Same structure as the sticker problem: ◆ is Ben’s ORIGINAL amount, ★ is what he gave away, ⬟ is the multiplier. The cousin’s amount now is ⬟×◆ (the multiplier times Ben’s original amount). The cousin’s starting amount is that minus the ★ received: ⬟×◆−★. Before picking an answer, always ask "which symbol is the ORIGINAL amount, and which is the amount that moved?"
2 Sticky Gap: Cube the Scale Factor for Volume
Problem 5
Two cubes are shown. Cube A has sides with length 3 millimeters. Cube B is 4 times as wide as Cube A. How many Cube A’s can fit inside Cube B?
AnswerC (64)
Why: Cube A’s volume is 3×3×3=27 cubic mm. Cube B’s side is 4×3=12 mm, so its volume is 12×12×12=1,728 cubic mm. 1,728÷27=64. The shortcut: when a cube’s side length scales by a factor (here 4), its VOLUME scales by that factor CUBED (4³=64) — not just the factor itself (12, too small) and not a surface-area-style calculation like 4×4×6=96 (that formula is for counting square faces, not fitting solid cubes inside).
Problem 6
Two cubes are shown. Cube A has sides with length 5 centimeters. Cube B is 3 times as wide as Cube A. How many Cube A’s can fit inside Cube B?
AnswerD (27)
Why: Cube A’s volume is 5×5×5=125 cubic cm. Cube B’s side is 3×5=15 cm, so its volume is 15×15×15=3,375 cubic cm. 3,375÷125=27. Same shortcut as before: cube the scale factor (3³=27) to get the volume ratio — squaring it (9) or multiplying it by 6 like a surface-area formula (54) both skip the actual 3D scaling.
2 Sticky Gap: Percent Up Then Down Always Nets a Decrease
Problem 7
A price is increased by 30%, then decreased by 30%. What is the percent increase or decrease of the final price compared to the original?
AnswerA (decrease by 9%)
Why: Start with 100: increasing by 30% gives 130. Decreasing THAT by 30% gives 130×0.7=91. Compared to the original 100, that’s a decrease of 9%, not "no change" and not an increase. A percent increase followed by an equal percent decrease never cancels out — the decrease is taken from a bigger number than the increase was, so the result always ends up a little BELOW where it started.
Problem 8
A population is increased by 10%, then decreased by 10%. What is the percent increase or decrease of the final population compared to the original?
AnswerB (decrease by 1%)
Why: Start with 100: increasing by 10% gives 110. Decreasing THAT by 10% gives 110×0.9=99. Compared to the original 100, that’s a decrease of 1%. Getting the SIZE of the change right (1%) but the DIRECTION wrong (calling it an increase) is the exact trap here — an equal percent up-then-down always lands slightly below the start, never above and never exactly even.
2 Habit Check: Watch MORE vs. LESS
Problem 9
Which fraction is MORE than 0.7?
AnswerC (7/9)
Why: 5/8=0.625, 2/3≈0.667, 3/5=0.6 — all three are actually LESS than 0.7. Only 7/9≈0.778 is MORE than 0.7. Converting every choice to a decimal before deciding (rather than guessing from how the fraction looks) is what catches this.
Problem 10
Which fraction is LESS than 0.35?
AnswerD (1/4)
Why: 3/8=0.375, 2/5=0.4, 5/12≈0.417 — all three are actually MORE than 0.35. Only 1/4=0.25 is LESS than 0.35. This is the same skill as the last problem with the direction word flipped from MORE to LESS — always re-read which direction the question is actually asking before picking.
2 Sticky Gap: Line Up the Decimal Point
Problem 11
What is the value of the expression 8.5 + 2.75?
Answer11.25
Why: Line up the decimal points before adding: write 8.5 as 8.50 so the tenths line up with tenths and the hundredths line up with hundredths. 8.50 + 2.75 = 11.25.
Problem 12
What is the value of the expression 4.6 + 3.25?
Answer7.85
Why: Line up the decimal points: 4.60 + 3.25 = 7.85. Writing the shorter decimal with a trailing zero first (4.6 → 4.60) helps make sure every column lines up with the matching place value.
