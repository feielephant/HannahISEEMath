7 9/27 Practice Test — Exact Repeat
Problem 1
Each letter represents a different digit from 1 through 9. AA + BB = CDE. What digit does C represent?
AnswerA (1)
Why: AA is 11×a and BB is 11×b, so the sum is 11×(a+b). Since a and b are different digits 1–9, a+b must be at least 10 for the result to have 3 digits, and at most 17. Every multiple of 11 from 110 to 187 starts with a 1, so C is always 1 — not 3, 7, or 9.
Problem 2
Jeremiah had □ pencils and then he gave ○ to Nathaniel. Now, Nathaniel has △ times as many pencils as Jeremiah had. Which expression represents the number of pencils Nathaniel had to begin with?
AnswerD (△×□−○)
Why: □ is Jeremiah’s ORIGINAL amount, ○ is what he gave away, △ is the multiplier. Nathaniel’s amount NOW equals △×□ (the multiplier times Jeremiah’s original amount). Nathaniel’s STARTING amount is that minus the ○ he received: △×□−○. Using ○ where □ belongs (or the reverse) is the trap in the other choices.
Problem 3
Two cubes are shown. Cube A has sides with length 2 millimeters. Cube B is 5 times as wide as Cube A. How many Cube A’s can fit inside Cube B?
AnswerC (125)
Why: Cube A’s volume is 2×2×2=8 cubic mm. Cube B’s side is 5×2=10 mm, so its volume is 10×10×10=1,000 cubic mm. 1,000÷8=125. The shortcut: cube the scale factor (5³=125) — 150 comes from a surface-area-style calculation (5×5×6), which counts faces, not how many solid cubes fit inside.
Problem 4
A salary is increased by 20% followed by a 20% decrease. What is the percent increase or percent decrease of the salary?
AnswerB (percent decrease by 4%)
Why: Start with 100: increasing by 20% gives 120. Decreasing THAT by 20% gives 120×0.8=96. Compared to the original 100, that’s a decrease of 4%. The size of the change (4%) is easy to get right, but the DIRECTION is the trap — an equal percent up-then-down always nets a decrease, never an increase, because the decrease is taken from the larger, already-increased amount.
Problem 5
Which fraction is more than 0.6?
AnswerD (3/4)
Why: 1/3≈0.33, 1/6≈0.17, and 2/5=0.4 are all LESS than 0.6. Only 3/4=0.75 is MORE than 0.6. Converting every choice to a decimal before deciding (instead of guessing from how the fraction looks) is what catches this — and re-reading "more than" instead of answering as if it said "less than."
Problem 6
What is the value of the expression 6.5 + 1.25?
AnswerD (7.75)
Why: Line up the decimal points before adding: write 6.5 as 6.50 so the tenths line up with tenths and the hundredths line up with hundredths. 6.50 + 1.25 = 7.75.

3 Earlier Errors — Cube-the-Scale-Factor (Book + 9/13)
Problem 8
A small cube has a base area that measures 4 square centimeters. A larger cube has a side length of 4 centimeters. How many small cubes could fit inside the larger cube?
AnswerC (8)
Why: A base area of 4 sq cm means the small cube’s side is 2 cm (since 2×2=4). The larger cube’s side is 4 cm, so the scale factor is 4÷2=2. Cube that scale factor for volume: 2³=8. Picking 2 — the scale factor itself, never cubed — is the exact trap from her original book answer.
Problem 9
The figure below is a large cube made up of smaller cubes. How many small cubes are used to make the larger cube?
AnswerB (125)
Why: The grid on the front and top faces shows a 5×5×5 cube: 5³=125. 150 comes from a surface-area-style calculation (5×5×6=150, counting the squares on all 6 faces) instead of counting the solid unit cubes inside — the same trap as the 9/13 and 9/27 versions of this problem.
Problem 10
Use the diagram of the cube to answer the question. How many small cubes are being used to build the large cube?
AnswerC (64)
Why: The grid on the front and top faces shows a 4×4×4 cube: 4³=64. 96 comes from a surface-area-style calculation (4×4×6=96) instead of counting solid unit cubes — the same trap that also came up on 9/5, at the book’s page 314, and again on 9/27.
9 Earlier Errors — Estimation: Matching Your Answer to the Right Bucket
Problem 11
A carton of milk contains 2.2 liters. Jake pours two glasses of milk, totaling ½ liter. How much milk is left in the carton?
AnswerC (more than 1½ and less than 2 liters)
Why: 2.2−0.5=1.7 liters left. 1.7 is more than 1.5 and less than 2, which is choice C. Choice A (less than ½ liter) would mean almost the whole carton is gone — a big underestimate, since less than a quarter of the milk was actually poured out. Missed on both the 8/30 and 9/13 sessions — the identical problem, twice.
Problem 12
What is a reasonable estimation for the value of 38×624÷18?
AnswerB (between 1,000 and 1,500)
Why: 38 is about 40, 624 is about 600, 18 stays about 18: 40×600÷18 ≈ 24,000÷18 ≈ 1,333, solidly inside 1,000–1,500. The exact value is about 1,317. Picking the next bucket up suggests the estimate itself was roughly on track, but wasn’t checked carefully against exactly which range it lands in. From the book, page 128.
Problem 13
Lucy went to the mall and bought the following items: a shirt for $34.49, three pairs of socks that each cost $2.99, and a pair of shoes that cost $67.49. What is the estimated total cost of Lucy’s items?
AnswerC (in between $110 and $115)
Why: Round each price: shirt ≈$34, socks ≈3×$3=$9, shoes ≈$67. Total ≈$34+$9+$67=$110. The exact total is $34.49+3($2.99)+$67.49=$110.95, which falls in the $110–115 bucket. Picking $100–105 is a full bucket too low — worth double-checking that each rounded piece actually adds up before choosing a range. From the book, page 128.
Problem 14
Devin bought six items costing $6.99, $3.49, $11.49, $5.49, $12.99, and $0.50. What is the estimated total cost of Devin’s items?
AnswerC (in between $40 and $45)
Why: Rounding each price ($7+$3.50+$11.50+$5.50+$13+$0.50=$41) lands in the $40–45 bucket. This is the clearest case of the pattern: her own scratch work reportedly landed on about 41 too — the ESTIMATE was right, but the wrong bucket got circled anyway. Always re-check: does my number actually fall inside the range I’m about to pick? From the book, page 130.
Problem 15
Lake Superior has an area of 82,100 km². Which of the following lakes has an area closest to 1/4 that of Lake Superior?
AnswerC (Lake Ontario, which has an area of 18,960 km²)
Why: 1/4 of 82,100 is 20,525 km². Check each choice’s DISTANCE from 20,525, not just which one looks close to the original 82,100: Vostok is 8,025 away, Balkhash is 4,125 away, Ontario is 1,565 away, Winnipeg is 3,989 away. Ontario is closest. Winnipeg is the biggest number and can feel like the closer one, but the actual distance from the target value shows Ontario wins. From the book, page 130.
Problem 16
What is a reasonable estimation for the value of 121×28÷64?
AnswerC (between 45 and 65)
Why: 121 is about 120, 28 stays about 28, 64 is about 60–65: 120×28÷64 ≈ 3,360÷64 ≈ 52.5, inside the 45–65 bucket. The exact value is about 52.9. Picking 65–75 overshoots by a full bucket — a sign the rounding or the division was done a little too roughly. From the book, page 131.
Problem 17
Ms. Giroux had 112 milk cartons in a refrigerator in the school cafeteria. She puts 31 more cases of milk cartons in the refrigerator. Each case contained 18 cartons. The expression 112+(31×18) represents the exact number of milk cartons in the refrigerator now. Which of these is the best estimate of the number of milk cartons in the refrigerator in the school cafeteria?
AnswerC (700)
Why: Round 31 to 30 and 18 to 20: 30×20=600, then add the 112 back in to get about 700. More precisely, 31×18=558, plus 112=670, which rounds to 700, not 600. Rounding both factors down is fine, but forgetting to add the 112 back in (or rounding it away) undershoots into the wrong bucket. From the 9/13 online session.
Problem 18
During a 5-month period, Anna’s water bills were $31.76, $46.22, $38.45, $51.98, and $45.62. What is the closest approximation of the total cost of Anna’s combined water bills during the 5-month period?
AnswerB ($210)
Why: Round each bill to the nearest dollar: 32+46+38+52+46=214, closest to $210. The exact total is $214.03. Picking $225 is a full $15 too high — likely from rounding too many bills UP (treating 38.45 as 40, 45.62 as 50) and stacking up the overestimate. From the 9/13 online session.
Problem 19
Josh did the problem shown with his calculator: 51×743÷25. What is a reasonable estimation for his answer?
AnswerB (between 1,200 and 2,000)
Why: 51 is about 50, 743 is about 750, 25 stays 25: 50×750÷25 = 37,500÷25 = 1,500, solidly inside 1,200–2,000. Picking 2,000–2,500 overshoots into the next bucket — worth double-checking the division step (37,500÷25) rather than estimating that part too. From the 9/5 online session.
