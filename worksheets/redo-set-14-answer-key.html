4 Know Your Metric & Percent Facts
Problem 1
1 kilogram is equal to how many grams?
Answer1000
Why: “Kilo-” always means 1,000 — worth knowing as a pure fact, not something to recalculate every time. 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams.
Problem 2
1 liter is equal to how many milliliters?
Answer1000
Why: Same fact, different unit: “kilo-” means 1,000, so 1 liter = 1,000 milliliters.
Problem 3
1 meter is equal to how many centimeters?
Answer100
Why: “Centi-” means 1/100, so it takes 100 centimeters to make 1 meter.
Problem 4
1 whole is equal to what percent?
Answer100
Why: 100% means the whole thing. This is the anchor fact every percent problem builds on — if a problem ever gives you “the whole,” that’s 100%.
2 Off by a Factor of 10 — One More Check
Problem 5
How many grams are in 3.6 kilograms?
AnswerA (3,600 grams)
Why: 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams, so 3.6 kg = 3.6×1,000 = 3,600 grams.
Problem 6
7,000 milliliters is equal to how many kiloliters?
AnswerB (0.007 kiloliters)
Why: 1 kiloliter = 1,000,000 milliliters, so 7,000÷1,000,000 = 0.007 kiloliters.
1 Multi-Step Conversion — One More Check
Problem 7
How many milligrams are in 0.08 kilograms?
AnswerC (80,000 milligrams)
Why: 0.08 kg = 80 g (kg→g is ×1,000). Then 80 g = 80,000 mg (g→mg is ×1,000 again). Two hops, not one.
2 Percent, Both Directions
Problem 8
What is 5% of 840?
AnswerD (42)
Why: 5% of 840 = 0.05×840 = 42.
Problem 9
8 is what percent of 40?
AnswerA (20%)
Why: 8÷40=0.2=20%.
1 Answering the Complement, Not the Target
Problem 10
A drawer has 15 socks, and 6 of them are white. If one sock is picked at random, what is the chance it will be white?
AnswerB (2/5)
Why: 6 of 15 is 6/15=2/5. Picking 3/5 would be the chance of NOT getting white (the complement) — always double-check which outcome the question actually asked for.
5 Sticky Gap — Estimation: Matching the Bucket
Problem 11
What is a reasonable estimation for the value of 42×518÷21?
AnswerC (between 1,000 and 1,500)
Why: 42 is about 40, 518 is about 500, 21 stays about 21…or more simply, 42÷21=2, so this is close to 2×518=1,036, solidly inside 1,000–1,500.
Problem 12
Maria bought a jacket for $45.49, two scarves that each cost $8.99, and a hat for $12.49. What is the estimated total cost of Maria’s items?
AnswerD (in between $75 and $80)
Why: Round each price: jacket ≈$45, scarves ≈2×$9=$18, hat ≈$12. Total ≈$45+$18+$12=$75. The exact total is $75.96, inside $75–80.
Problem 13
A reservoir holds 64,400 gallons of water. Which of the following tanks holds an amount closest to 1/4 that of the reservoir?
AnswerA (Tank R, which holds 15,600 gallons)
Why: 1/4 of 64,400 is 16,100 gallons. Check the DISTANCE from 16,100 for each choice: Tank R is only 500 away, the closest of the four.
Problem 14
During a 4-month period, Jake’s phone bills were $52.25, $48.90, $61.15, and $55.40. What is the closest approximation of the total cost of Jake’s combined phone bills?
AnswerB ($220)
Why: Round each bill to the nearest dollar: 52+49+61+55=217, closest to $220.
Problem 15
Mr. Alvarez had 95 notebooks in a supply closet. He adds 24 more boxes of notebooks, with each box containing 15 notebooks. The expression 95+(24×15) represents the exact number of notebooks now. Which of these is the best estimate of the number of notebooks in the supply closet?
AnswerC (500)
Why: 24×15=360, plus 95=455. Between 400 and 500, 455 is closer to 500 (only 45 away, vs. 55 away from 400).
