3 Number Theory & Estimation
Problem 1
Ms. Petrov had 95 milk cartons in a refrigerator in the school cafeteria. She puts 29 more cases of milk cartons in the refrigerator. Each case contained 19 cartons.
The expression 95 + (29 × 19) represents the exact number of milk cartons in the refrigerator now.
Which of these is the best estimate of the number of milk cartons in the refrigerator in the school cafeteria?
AnswerB
Why: Rounding each number to a friendly value: 95≈100, 29≈30, 19≈20, so 30×20=600, plus 100 gives an estimate of 700. The exact value (95+29×19=646) confirms 700 is the closest estimate among the choices.
Problem 2
Which number is divisible by 12?
AnswerA
Why: A number divisible by 12 must be divisible by both 3 and 4. 336÷12=28 exactly. 124, 214, and 442 all leave a remainder when divided by 12.
Problem 3
During a 5-month period, Malik's electric bills were $42.15, $55.80, $29.34, $61.05, and $48.72. What is the closest approximation of the total cost of Malik's combined electric bills during the 5-month period?
AnswerD
Why: The exact total is $42.15+$55.80+$29.34+$61.05+$48.72=$237.06, which is closest to $240 (about $2.94 away) compared to $230 (about $7.06 away).
3 Fractions & Ratios
Problem 4
The width of a rectangle is Y meters. Which statement correctly compares the value of Y to the length of the rectangle, where
Y = 3⁄5 × length?
AnswerB
Why: The equation Y = 3⁄5 × length directly states that Y is always 3⁄5 times the length — no calculation needed, just reading the relationship as given.
Problem 5
Mika has two equal-sized bags of birdseed.
• The first bag is n⁄5 full.
• The second bag is 1⁄4 full.
The birdseed from these bags will be combined by pouring the birdseed from the first bag into the second bag.
Which statement best describes the amount of birdseed once the bags are combined?
AnswerA
Why: If n=4: 4⁄5 + 1⁄4 = 16⁄20 + 5⁄20 = 21⁄20, which is more than 1 whole bag, so it will not all fit. Checking the others: n=1 gives 9⁄20=0.45, which is NOT less than 1⁄3≈0.33 (choice A is false); n=2 gives 13⁄20=0.65, NOT less than 1⁄2 (choice B is false); n=3 gives 17⁄20=0.85, which is less than 1, so it WOULD fit (choice C is false).
Problem 6
A recipe uses 936 milliliters of milk. Yara wants to make 1⁄4 of the recipe. If she cuts the recipe into fourths, how many milliliters of milk should she use?
AnswerA
Why: 936 × 1⁄4 = 936 ÷ 4 = 234 milliliters.
3 Patterns & Algebraic Thinking
Problem 7
The length and width of the array shown represent two factors of a number.
AnswerB
Why: The reference array is 4 rows × 12 columns = 48. Choice A (6×8=48) has the same product but a different factor pair — that's the same number, shown with two different factors. B (4×11=44), C (5×9=45), and D (7×7=49) all give different products entirely.
Problem 8
Two numbers are greater than 1. One is a prime number and the other is an odd number. What will always describe the product of the two numbers?
AnswerC
Why: Any product of two whole numbers that are each greater than 1 is always composite, since it always has at least the factors 1, itself, and the two numbers multiplied together — that's true regardless of whether the individual numbers are prime, odd, or even. (The product's parity actually varies: prime=2 with odd=3 gives an even product 6, while prime=3 with odd=5 gives an odd product 15 — so 'odd' and 'even' can't be the guaranteed answer.)
Problem 9
Which expression is equivalent to 12 × (14 + 22)?
AnswerD
Why: The distributive property says a × (b + c) = (a × b) + (a × c). Applying it here: 12 × (14 + 22) = (12 × 14) + (12 × 22).
4 Geometry & Measurement
Problem 10
The shaded area on the square represents the part of a rectangular floor that has tile already installed. Each small square on the floor has the same dimensions.
What percentage of the floor has tile already installed?
AnswerD
Why: The grid has 25 small squares total. The 5 white squares (forming a plus shape in the middle) are NOT yet tiled, so 20 of the 25 squares are tiled: 20÷25 = 80%.
Problem 11
Priya wants to cover a sidewalk with pavers. Her sidewalk is 120 inches long and 36 inches wide. Each tile is 6 inches long and 4 inches wide. She started to lay the pavers to see how many she would need.
If she knows how to find the area of a rectangle, how can Priya determine how many pavers she will need without laying them all on the sidewalk? (A = l×w)
AnswerA
Why: Dividing each dimension of the sidewalk by the matching dimension of one tile gives the number of tiles that fit along each direction: (120÷6)×(36÷4) = 20×9 = 180 pavers.
Problem 12
The floor of Room A is in the shape of a square and has an area of 100 feet2. The floor of Room B is in the shape of a rectangle and has a length of 6 feet and width of 14 feet. Which statement correctly compares the floors in the two rooms?
AnswerA
Why: Room A's side is √100=10 ft, so its perimeter is 4×10=40 ft. Room B's perimeter is 2×(6+14)=40 ft — the perimeters are equal. The areas are NOT equal (Room A=100 ft², Room B=6×14=84 ft²).
Problem 13
The base of a triangle is 8 meters (m). Which statement correctly compares the value of A to the base and height of the triangle? (A = 1⁄2 × base × height)
AnswerD
Why: Substituting base=8 into A = 1⁄2 × base × height gives A = 1⁄2 × 8 × height = 4 × height, so A is always 4 times the height.
2 Data Analysis
Problem 14
The table shows the number of votes for different school colors.
Based on the information in the table, what is the most reasonable prediction of the number of votes for maroon out of the next 30 votes?
AnswerC
Why: Total votes so far: 12+18+6+24=60. Maroon's share is 18⁄60 = 3⁄10. Applying that same proportion to the next 30 votes: 3⁄10 × 30 = 9.
Problem 15
The stem-and-leaf plot shows the high temperatures in a town for two weeks, where 4|0 represents 40.
Based on the data in the stem-and-leaf plot, which conclusion is true?
AnswerB
Why: Reading the plot: 40,44,44,49,51,55,58,58,62,64,67,71,71,71 — exactly three days (71,71,71) reach at least 71°F. 49°F occurs only once (not 2 days), the lowest temperature is 40°F (so it was NOT at least 55°F every day), and 67°F occurs only once (not 3 days).
