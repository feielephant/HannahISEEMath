4 Multi-Step Area & Perimeter Strategy
Problem 1
The perimeter of a square is 32 centimeters. If a rectangle with a width of 4 centimeters has the same area as the square, what is the length of the rectangle in centimeters?
Answer16
Why: Side of square = 32 ÷ 4 = 8. Area = 8 × 8 = 64. Length of rectangle = 64 ÷ 4 = 16. The key step is finding the square's area FIRST — you can't get the rectangle's length by operating on the perimeter directly.
Problem 2
The perimeter of a square is 40 centimeters. If a rectangle with a width of 5 centimeters has the same area as the square, what is the length of the rectangle in centimeters?
Answer20
Why: Side of square = 40 ÷ 4 = 10. Area = 10 × 10 = 100. Length of rectangle = 100 ÷ 5 = 20.
Problem 3
Maria wants to cover a path with paving tiles. The path is 96 inches long and 60 inches wide. Each tile is 6 inches long and 5 inches wide. If she knows how to find the area of a rectangle, how can Maria determine how many tiles she needs without laying them all on the path? (A = l × w)
AnswerB
Why: The number of tiles needed = (path length ÷ tile length) × (path width ÷ tile width) = (96÷6) × (60÷5) = 16 × 12 = 192 tiles. Subtracting or multiplying the raw dimensions (A, C, D) doesn't correspond to "how many tiles fit."
Problem 4
An array with 7 rows and 9 columns has 63 squares in total. Which pair of dimensions describes a DIFFERENT array with the same total number of squares?
AnswerA
Why: 3 × 21 = 63, matching the original array's total. Check every choice by multiplying: 5×12=60, 6×11=66, 8×8=64 — none of those match 63.
4 “Always True?” Logic
Problem 5
Two numbers are each greater than 1. One number is even, and the other number is a multiple of 5. What will always be true about the product of the two numbers?
AnswerB
Why: The even number always contributes a factor of 2; the multiple of 5 always contributes a factor of 5. So the product is always divisible by 2 × 5 = 10. Test it: 2×5=10, 4×15=60, 6×25=150 — all multiples of 10. (D fails: 2×5=10, which is not greater than 20.)
Problem 6
Which statement is true?
AnswerB
Why: A rhombus is a 4-sided shape with all sides equal. A square has all sides equal, so every square meets that definition — a square is always a rhombus. But not every rhombus has 90° angles, so a rhombus is not always a square (that reverses the true relationship, same trap as "a rectangle is always a square").
Problem 7
Room A is a square with an area of 121 square feet. Room B is a rectangle with a length of 20 feet and a width of 5 feet. Which statement correctly compares the two rooms?
AnswerB
Why: Room A: side = √121 = 11, perimeter = 4×11 = 44 ft. Room B: area = 20×5 = 100 sq ft, perimeter = 2(20+5) = 50 ft. Room A actually has the LARGER area (121 > 100) but the SMALLER perimeter (44 < 50) — proof that a bigger area does not guarantee a bigger perimeter. That's exactly why D is a trap.
Problem 8
A number is chosen that is a prime number greater than 2. What can you always say about that number?
AnswerA
Why: 2 is the only even prime number. Once you require the prime to be greater than 2, every remaining prime (3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ...) is odd. (D fails because 5 itself is a prime greater than 2 and IS a multiple of 5.)
4 Organize the Data Before Computing
Problem 9
A set of numbers shows the points scored by 7 players in a game: 14, 8, 22, 5, 19, 11, 17. What is the median number of points scored?
Answer14
Why: Sort first: 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 19, 22. With 7 values, the median is the 4th one once sorted — 14. (Averaging any values in their original, unsorted order is not the median.)
Problem 10
The table shows the number of visitors to a museum on each day of one week. What is the median number of visitors?
Answer120
Why: Values as listed by day: 120, 95, 150, 80, 135, 200, 110. Sort them first: 80, 95, 110, 120, 135, 150, 200. The median (middle of 7 sorted values) is 120 — the Monday value, which is a coincidence of this data, not a shortcut for future problems.
Problem 11
The stem-and-leaf plot shows the ages of 12 people at a family reunion, where 3|4 represents 34. Based on the plot, how many people are exactly 42 years old?
Answer1
Why: The stem "4" row reads: 2, 6, 6, 6 — that's FOUR different ages: 42, 46, 46, and 46. Only one leaf is a "2," so only one person is exactly 42. (Three people are 46 — don't mix up "three leaves in the row" with "three people at one age" unless the leaves themselves repeat.)
Problem 12
In a survey of 60 people, 18 said they prefer tea over coffee. Based on this survey, how many of the next 40 people surveyed would be expected to prefer tea?
Answer12
Why: Build the ratio first: 18 out of 60 prefer tea, which is 18/60 = 0.3 (30%). Apply that same rate to 40 people: 0.3 × 40 = 12. Subtracting or comparing the two raw numbers (60 and 18, or 18 and 40) directly does not answer a prediction question — you need the rate.
3 Number-Line Tick Counting
Problem 13
What value is the arrow pointing to on the number line? (0 to 2, marked off in equal ticks)
Answer1.4
Why: Count the ticks between the labels first: there are 10 equal spaces between 0 and 2, so each tick is worth 2 ÷ 10 = 0.2. The arrow sits on the 7th tick from 0: 7 × 0.2 = 1.4.
Problem 14
What value is the arrow pointing to on the number line? (3 to 5, marked off in equal ticks)
Answer3.75 (3¾)
Why: There are 8 equal spaces between 3 and 5 (a span of 2), so each tick is worth 2 ÷ 8 = 0.25. The arrow sits on the 3rd tick from 3: 3 + (3 × 0.25) = 3.75.
Problem 15
What value is the arrow pointing to on the number line? (10 to 16, marked off in equal ticks)
Answer14.5
Why: There are 12 equal spaces between 10 and 16 (a span of 6), so each tick is worth 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5. The arrow sits on the 9th tick from 10: 10 + (9 × 0.5) = 14.5.
3 Probability: The Right Category
Problem 16
A bag contains 15 marbles: 6 red, 4 blue, and 5 green. If one marble is drawn at random, what is the probability that it will NOT be red?
AnswerB (3/5)
Why: "Not red" means blue or green: 4 + 5 = 9 out of 15, which reduces to 3/5. Choice A (2/5) is 6/15 — the probability of drawing RED, the opposite of what's asked. Always underline the word NOT and double-check which group you're counting.
Problem 17
A spinner is divided into 10 equal sections. 3 sections are yellow and the rest are green. What is the probability that the spinner will land on a section that is NOT yellow?
AnswerB (7/10)
Why: 10 total sections, 3 yellow, so 10−3 = 7 are NOT yellow: 7/10. Choice A (3/10) is the probability of landing on yellow — the trap.
Problem 18
A raffle sold 25 tickets in total, and 10 of them are winning tickets. What is the probability that a randomly chosen ticket is a LOSING ticket?
AnswerB (3/5)
Why: 25 − 10 = 15 losing tickets, out of 25 total: 15/25 = 3/5. Choice A (2/5) is 10/25, the probability of a WINNING ticket — again, the complement trap.
3 Spatial & Grid Counting
Problem 19
The grid below has 20 equal squares. What percentage of the grid is NOT shaded?
Answer25%
Why: Count the un-shaded (white) squares one at a time, row by row — there are 5 out of 20 total. 5/20 = 1/4 = 25%. It helps to physically mark each un-shaded square with a small tally as you count, rather than eyeballing the total.
Problem 20
A rectangular box is built out of identical unit cubes, with no gaps. It is 5 cubes long, 3 cubes wide, and 4 cubes tall. How many unit cubes make up the entire box?
Answer60
Why: Volume = length × width × height = 5 × 3 × 4 = 60. Double-check that you're using the correct number for each of the three dimensions rather than swapping one for another.
Problem 21
A tower is built by stacking identical layers of unit cubes. Each layer is a 4-by-4 square of cubes, and there are 5 layers stacked directly on top of each other. How many unit cubes make up the tower in total?
Answer80
Why: One layer = 4 × 4 = 16 cubes. 5 layers = 16 × 5 = 80 cubes total.
2 Distributive Property
Problem 22
Which expression is equivalent to 14 × (12 + 25)?
AnswerB
Why: The distributive property multiplies 14 by BOTH terms inside the parentheses: 14×(12+25) = (14×12)+(14×25). Choice A only multiplies 14 by the 12 and leaves the 25 untouched — that changes the value of the expression.
Problem 23
Which expression is equivalent to 20 × (9 + 16)?
AnswerB
Why: 20×(9+16) = (20×9)+(20×16). Both terms inside the parentheses need their own multiplication by 20.
4 Quick Check: Redo Set 7 Skills
Problem 24
Which number is divisible by both 3 and 4?
AnswerC (336)
Why: Check both rules for every choice: 342 (digit sum 9, divisible by 3; but last 2 digits 42÷4 is not whole — fails 4). 316 (last 2 digits 16÷4=4, divisible by 4; but digit sum 10 — fails 3). 336 (digit sum 12, divisible by 3; last 2 digits 36÷4=9, divisible by 4 — passes both). 322 (fails both).
Problem 25
Denver is 2 hours behind Chicago. If a train leaves Chicago at 9:00 A.M. and arrives in Denver 6 hours later (Denver local time), what time is it in Denver when the train arrives?
AnswerB (1:00 P.M.)
Why: Convert the departure time to Denver time first: 9:00 A.M. Chicago − 2 hours = 7:00 A.M. Denver. Then add the 6-hour travel time: 7:00 A.M. + 6 hours = 1:00 P.M.
Problem 26
The perimeter of the largest polygon is 34 centimeters. The perimeter of the square is 24 centimeters. Use the figure to answer the question. What is the perimeter of the triangle?
Answer22
Why: Square's side = 24 ÷ 4 = 6. The shared side between the square and triangle is counted once in the "largest polygon" outline but is a full side (counted twice, once per shape) when you separate the two shapes: triangle's perimeter = 34 − 24 + (2 × 6) = 22.
Problem 27
Priya did the problem shown with her calculator.
(68 × 512) / 20
What is a reasonable estimation for her answer?
AnswerC
Why: Exact value: 68 × 512 ÷ 20 = 34,816 ÷ 20 = 1,740.8, which falls in the "1,600 to 2,000" bucket. A quick estimate: 70 × 500 ÷ 20 = 35,000 ÷ 20 = 1,750, landing in the same bucket.
